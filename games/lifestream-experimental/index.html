One world. One disturbance.

Tend the lifestream.

Begin lives around the world ring. Follow the moving cloud, shelter energy through living people, and help the world endure the disturbance.

Plant with care. The first embryo needs 12 energy within 6 seconds. Later generations need more, but create more in return. Seeds are unlimited. Failed embryos destroy all energy they absorbed.
Read the current. Crowded places offer support. Sparse places give successful lives more room to grow.
Weather the disturbance. Gray warnings precede temporary sinks. A final ring of sinks tests the world before every remaining person is gathered in light.

Create as much new energy as you can. Completed lives are a secondary result. Pause whenever you like.

A few quiet rules

Watch where life can grow.

Tap the inner ring to plant. Move the pointer, or use the arrow controls, to inspect the supply at a location. Planting closes six seconds before gathering.

Souls fill gradually. Embryos absorb nearby energy until they hold the requirement shown beside the seeds. The center fills as energy arrives; the outer arc shows time left. A filled embryo still develops for six seconds. Failed embryos destroy what they absorbed. Planting too many can make all of them fail.

Life advances. As the world creates energy, new seeds require more soul energy and grow into larger, more productive people. Each generation keeps the traits it had when planted.

Space enriches life. People create more energy when fewer others share their surroundings. Their glow reflects that growth; everyone still has a finite lifetime.

Create sanctuaries. Every 150 energy created earns one persistent shield. Right-click or Control-click the world to deploy quickly, tap the Sanctuary button and then the world, or drag the button onto the world. A sanctuary neutralizes any sink touching its sparkling field, including partial overlaps; overlapping fields do not stack.

Violet means an active sink. Gray arcs warn of arrival. Color drains in the final moment before activation. A flash marks activation; the black disk consumes unprotected cloud energy and the violet arc shows time left. Neutralized sinks remain gray. The outermost cloud is safe from sinks. Only embryos pull energy out of it; once between the planes, that energy is exposed. The cloud moves clockwise, faster near the outside.

After the final ring, calm returns. The remaining people rise into the cloud, keep their remaining energy, and count as completed lives. Failed embryos do not count.

Your time is yours. Space pauses; switching tabs pauses automatically. Art view hides the interface and continues at the current pace; Esc, F, or double-click exits.
